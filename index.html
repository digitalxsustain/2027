Cross-sectoral Dialogue for Digital Futures

17–18 March 2027 | USquare, Brussels

About

DIGITAL X SUSTAIN is a two-day, hands-on event on the sustainability of our digital and physical worlds.

While the digital transition is seen as a motor to enable green transitions, this perspective also has some critics. Researchers and civil society organizations have been drawing attention to potential discrepancies between digital and sustainability goals. They are notably highlighting questions around public participation, social impacts, and the environmental impact of digitalization, including electricity consumption, water consumption, extraction of rare materials, and e-waste, all of which require public discourse and policy reforms.

While adoption and criticism occur in parallel, proponents and opponents rarely meet in dialogue. This limits creative innovations that can be born out of constructive disagreements. We aim to bring various perspectives into focus by inviting stakeholders from different sectors to engage in dialogue.

We invite practitioners from public, private, and associative sectors, as well as researchers, artists, designers, and residents, who engage in projects that foster or question the sustainability issues related to the internet and digital technologies, to present their work and ideas in traditional and innovative formats.

Formats

You can submit your work in various formats. We encourage diverse modes of expression and dialogue alongside more traditional ones.

You are expected to submit a 500-word abstract outlining your contributions and presentation approach. Submissions will be reviewed based on relevance to conference themes and contribution to a diverse, public-facing program.

Accepted abstracts appear in the conference program, with publication opportunities to be confirmed closer to the event. This is an on-site event; authors need to present in person.

Themes

Share a project, a tool, a public policy… or even a failure that offers valuable lessons on one of the three main themes related to digital sustainability:

01Materials, Resources, and Infrastructure

•

Digital Infrastructure and Networks: Antennas, cables, and mobile networks consume energy, materials, and public space, and they are often designed to handle peak demand. Have you worked on network energy consumption, infrastructure sharing, the decommissioning of old networks, or aging equipment?

•

Green IT and IT for Green: Have you measured the environmental impact of a digital service, or verified whether a “smart” project has actually delivered the promised savings?

•

Circularity and Responsible Procurement: Have you drafted specifications requiring refurbished or repairable equipment, or are you managing or participating in a repair initiative?

02Design and Practices

•

Digital Sufficiency: Have you reduced the amount of data you create, use, or store, or extended the lifespan of your devices? Are you developing or working on digital services and devices that can function with less?

•

Alternative AI: Have you run a small AI model locally, or decided not to use AI at all? Are you developing or exploring alternatives to mainstream AI?

03Public Policy, Governance, and Participation

•

Sustainability Policies for the Digital and Telecom Sectors: Are you working on sustainability rules, standards, requirements, or goals for the digital and telecom sectors? What happens when they are put into practice?

•

Resilience, robustness, and sovereignty: How can digital systems continue to function in the face of energy shortages, climate shocks, or supply disruptions? What does digital sovereignty mean to you in practice?

•

Net Neutrality and Fairness: Are you working on how regulations and protocols determine which traffic takes priority and at what speed?

•

Communities, Participation, and the Commons: Are you part of a community network or a digital cooperative, or do you work with communities to make technical democracy possible?
